<Part id="learn">
  ### The Investment Summary

  The Investment Summary is your whole pitch in under a page. It states:

  - your recommendation (buy, sell, or hold), <GlossaryTerm id="target-price">target price</GlossaryTerm>, and <GlossaryTerm id="upside">upside</GlossaryTerm> or <GlossaryTerm id="downside">downside</GlossaryTerm>
  - your three thesis pillars, one or two sentences each
  - the main risk and why you're still confident

  Write it last, after everything else is done, even though it comes third in your report, right after Industry Overview and before Valuation.

  ### Thesis pillars

  A <GlossaryTerm id="thesis-pillar">thesis pillar</GlossaryTerm> is one main reason for your recommendation. Three is the usual number: enough to be convincing, few enough to remember. A strong pillar has three parts:

  1. **A claim:** "Membership fees give the company steady, growing income."
  2. **Evidence:** a number from your research, with its source.
  3. **Why it matters for the stock:** how it affects future earnings or the P/E the market will pay.

  The best pillars point to something the market may be missing or underrating. If everyone already agrees, it's probably already in the price.

  ### Catalysts

  A <GlossaryTerm id="catalyst">catalyst</GlossaryTerm> is an event that could make the market notice your thesis, such as an earnings report, a product launch, or a new store opening. Name one or two, and say roughly when they'll happen.

  ### Investment Risks

  Every investment can go wrong, and judges trust analysts who say how. For each of your three to five biggest risks:

  - **Describe it specifically.** "Competition" is vague; "Sam's Club cutting prices in Costco's biggest markets" is specific.
  - **Rate it:** how likely, and how much damage if it happens.
  - **Name a <GlossaryTerm id="mitigant">mitigant</GlossaryTerm>:** something that reduces the risk or its damage.

  Your <GlossaryTerm id="10-k">10-K</GlossaryTerm>'s Item 1A lists the risks the company itself worries about. Start there, then add any you found yourself. The [risk matrix](/tools/risk-matrix) tool helps you rate them.

  ### ESG considerations

  ESG stands for environmental, social, and governance. Analysts ask whether these issues could affect the company's value, not whether the company is "good." Pick the one or two that matter most for your company:

  - **Environmental:** energy use, emissions, waste, climate risks to its operations
  - **Social:** how it treats employees, customers, and suppliers; product safety; data privacy
  - **Governance:** how the company is run: its board, executive pay, and shareholder rights

  Find material in the company's <GlossaryTerm id="sustainability-report">sustainability report</GlossaryTerm> and its 10-K. Explain how each issue could help or hurt the business.

  ### AI considerations

  YIS asks every entry: how does the development of artificial intelligence affect opportunity or risk for your company? Cover both sides:

  - **Opportunity:** Could AI lower costs, improve products, or help the company reach customers?
  - **Risk:** Could AI help competitors, change how customers buy, or make the product less necessary?

  Look for what management says about AI in the 10-K and in <GlossaryTerm id="earnings-call">earnings calls</GlossaryTerm>, and back up each point with evidence.

  A reminder about your own work: you can use AI tools to explain concepts and point you toward sources, but YIS prohibits using AI to write any part of your submission, including this section.
</Part>

  The case an analyst arguing for the stock might make:

  | Loyal members create steady, growing income | 92.3% renewal rate in the US and Canada; membership fees grew 11.0% to $5.9 billion | Fees are predictable and high-profit, which supports earnings in a weak economy |

  ### Costco's main risks, with mitigants

  <div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="Costco's main risks with mitigants" tabindex="0">

  | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigant |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | The stock is already expensive: a P/E of about 48 leaves little room if growth slows | Medium | High | Steady earnings growth could "grow into" the price |
  | Competition from Sam's Club and online retailers like Amazon | Medium | Medium | Scale and member loyalty are hard to copy |
  | Fewer members renew, cutting fee income | Low | High | Renewal is high: 92.3% in the US and Canada, 89.8% worldwide |

  The last three risks come straight from Item 1A of Costco's 10-K. The first comes from the valuation in module 5.

  ### ESG: questions an analyst would ask about Costco

  - **Social:** Costco depends on fast, friendly service in huge warehouses. How does it pay and keep its employees, and how does that affect costs?
  - **Environmental:** Its warehouses use a lot of energy and its products travel long distances. Are there climate or energy costs that could hurt margins?
  - **Governance:** Is executive pay tied to long-term results?

  **A finished ESG point (social)**

  > Costco's low-cost, high-volume model depends on its employees. It employed 355,000 people at the end of fiscal 2026, raised the top of its US and Canadian wage scales by $1.00 an hour in March 2026, and brought its average US hourly wage to about $33.00. About 94% of US and Canadian employees with at least a year of service stayed with the company. Higher pay adds cost, but high retention can lower hiring and training costs and support the service members pay for. (Source: Costco 10-K, fiscal 2026.)

  Notice it states facts with a source, then explains how the issue could help or hurt the business, rather than praising the company.

  ### AI: both sides for Costco

  - **Risk (from Costco's 10-K):** AI tools could change shopping habits, intensify competition, or show inaccurate pricing, and some rivals are adopting AI faster.
  - **Opportunity (to research):** Could AI help Costco manage inventory, set prices, or improve its online shopping? Look for what management says in earnings calls before claiming it.

  ### A sample Investment Summary (teaching example only, using the prices from module 5)

  > We rate Costco Wholesale (Nasdaq: COST) a Hold, with a target price of $1,027.80, 3.0% above the current $997.42 (as of October 27, 2026). Costco is an exceptional business: 92.3% of its US and Canadian members renew, membership fees grew 11.0% to $5.9 billion in fiscal 2026, and it holds $20.2 billion in cash against $6.2 billion in debt. However, at 48 times earnings, the market already prices in much of this strength. Our target assumes 10% EPS growth and a P/E of 45. A P/E of 50 would imply 14.5% upside, while 40 would imply 8.4% downside, so the recommendation depends mainly on whether Costco can keep its premium valuation.

  Notice it's short, leads with the recommendation and numbers, gives the strongest evidence, and names the key uncertainty. YIS's first page allows Buy, Sell, or Hold; GHIC's rule in module 5 tells you which to use.

  2. Does your Investment Summary state the recommendation, target price, and upside in its first two sentences?
  3. Is each risk specific, rated, and paired with a mitigant?
