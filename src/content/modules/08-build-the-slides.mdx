<Part id="why">
  The presentation is worth {categoryPoints('presentation')} points on its own, and your slides also appear in your video, which is worth another {categoryPoints('video')}. That's half your score riding on how well you present, not just what you found.

  The biggest presentation line in the rubric asks whether you "use bullets and elaborate on those topics." Your slides should give judges the key points at a glance, while you supply the explanation out loud. Slides packed with paragraphs fail at both.
</Part>

<Part id="learn">
  ### The deck is a summary of your report

  Your slides cover the same argument as your report, in the same order, with fewer words. Don't add new analysis here; if something's important, it belongs in the report too.

  ### What every deck must include

  The title slide carries the same information as your report's first page:

  <ul>
    {firstPageFields.map((field) => <li>{field.label}</li>)}
  </ul>

  The deck must also cover every required section:

  <ol>
    {requiredSections.map((section) => (
      <li>{section.label}{section.detail ? `: ${section.detail}` : ''}</li>
    ))}
  </ol>

  ### How many slides?

  Your video has to be under {limits.videoMaxMinutes} minutes, and you'll present these slides in it. Plan on about 12 to 15 slides: one for the title, one for your recommendation, roughly one for each required section, and one to close. Fewer, stronger slides beat many rushed ones.

  ### Bullets you can talk to

  - **Three to five bullets per slide**, each a short phrase, not a full sentence
  - **One idea per slide:** the slide title should state the point, like "Members keep coming back," not just "Membership"
  - **Elaborate out loud:** each bullet is a prompt for what you'll say, not a script to read
  - **Big text:** if it's too small to read on a phone screen in your video, it's too small

  ### Show numbers as visuals

  YIS asks you to use slides as a visual for financial information, like {guidance.slides.financialVisuals.join(' and ')}. Turn your key numbers into a simple chart: a bar chart for revenue over time, a table for your valuation math, a pie chart for a revenue breakdown. Give each chart a clear title and a source line.

  ### Keep the design simple

  Use one template, two fonts at most, and the same colors throughout. Plain backgrounds make charts easier to read. Avoid animations and transitions; they don't help in a recorded video.

  ### File format

  YIS accepts PowerPoint or PDF, and does not accept Keynote. If you build in Google Slides, download it as a PowerPoint file (File, then Download, then Microsoft PowerPoint) and open it once to check nothing moved. Name it <code class="[overflow-wrap:anywhere]">{slidesFileName}</code>.
</Part>

<Part id="do">
  1. **<TemplateLink id="slides" />** and click **Make a copy**. Replace every bracketed prompt with your own content.
  2. **Plan your slides in a list first**, one point per slide, following your report's order.
  3. **Build the title slide** with every required field.
  4. **Write a point-stating title for each slide**, then three to five short bullets.
  5. **Turn your key numbers into charts or tables**, each with a title and source.
  6. **Check that every required section** appears somewhere in the deck.
  7. **Practice talking through it once out loud.** Cut or split any slide you can't explain in about 30 to 60 seconds.
  8. **Download it as PowerPoint**, named <code class="[overflow-wrap:anywhere]">{slidesFileName}</code>, and open it to check the layout.
  9. **Tick off module 8** in your [progress tracker](/learn#progress) and send the deck to your mentor.

  **Deliverable:** your complete slide deck. Due by <time datetime={new Date(frontmatter.due).toISOString().slice(0, 10)}>{formatDue(new Date(frontmatter.due))}</time>.
</Part>

<Part id="check">
  1. Does the title slide have every first-page field, with a dated price?
  2. Is every required section covered?
  3. Does every slide title state a point?
  4. Are there five or fewer short bullets on every slide?
  5. Are your key numbers shown as charts or tables, with sources?
  6. Do the numbers match your report exactly?
  7. Is it saved as PowerPoint or PDF, not Keynote, with the correct name?

  **Common mistakes:** pasting paragraphs from the report onto slides, tiny text, charts without titles or sources, numbers that don't match the report, and leaving the ESG or AI section out of the deck.
</Part>
