<Part id="why">
  A great company isn't always a great investment. If everyone already knows how good it is, its stock price may already reflect that, leaving little room to rise. <GlossaryTerm id="valuation">Valuation</GlossaryTerm> is how you decide whether the price is too high, too low, or about right.

  Your valuation produces the two numbers judges look for first: your <GlossaryTerm id="target-price">target price</GlossaryTerm> and the percent <GlossaryTerm id="upside">upside</GlossaryTerm> or <GlossaryTerm id="downside">downside</GlossaryTerm> from today's price. Both go on the first page of your report and your title slide, and your whole recommendation rests on them.
</Part>

<Part id="learn">
  ### The P/E ratio

  The <GlossaryTerm id="pe-ratio">price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio</GlossaryTerm> tells you how much investors pay for each dollar of a company's yearly profit.

  $$
  \text{P/E} = \frac{\text{Share price}}{\text{Earnings per share (EPS)}}
  $$

  A stock at $50 with <GlossaryTerm id="eps">EPS</GlossaryTerm> of $2.50 has a P/E of 20: investors pay $20 for every $1 of earnings. A high P/E usually means investors expect fast growth or see the company as unusually safe. A low P/E can mean the opposite, or that the market is overlooking something.

  P/E only works when <GlossaryTerm id="earnings">earnings</GlossaryTerm> are positive. That's why module 1 asked you to pick a profitable company.

  ### The valuation in three steps

  **Step 1: Forecast next year's EPS.** Start from the most recent full-year <GlossaryTerm id="diluted-eps">diluted EPS</GlossaryTerm> (module 4). Then decide how fast it will grow, using:

  - its growth over the past few years
  - what management says to expect in <GlossaryTerm id="earnings-call">earnings calls</GlossaryTerm> and reports
  - what you learned about the industry and competition in module 3

  Write down your growth assumption and your reason for it. Judges care more about a well-reasoned assumption than a precise one.

  **Step 2: Choose a target P/E.** This is the P/E you think the stock deserves in a year. Two ways to anchor it:

  - **<GlossaryTerm id="comparable-companies">Comparable companies</GlossaryTerm> ("comps"):** the P/E of three or four similar companies, using their average or median
  - **The company's own history:** the P/E it has usually traded at over recent years

  Then adjust. If your company grows faster or has a stronger <GlossaryTerm id="moat">moat</GlossaryTerm> than its peers, a higher P/E can be justified, but you have to say why.

  **Step 3: Calculate the target price and upside.**

  $$
  \text{Target price} = \text{Forecast EPS} \times \text{Target P/E}
  $$

  $$
  \text{Upside} = \frac{\text{Target price} - \text{Current price}}{\text{Current price}}
  $$

  A negative upside is downside, and it points toward a sell.

  ### Test your assumptions

  Your target price is only as good as your two inputs. A <GlossaryTerm id="sensitivity-table">sensitivity table</GlossaryTerm> shows what happens if you're a little wrong: it calculates the target price for a range of EPS forecasts and P/Es. If small changes flip your answer from buy to sell, say so honestly in your report. The [P/E calculator](/tools/pe-calculator) on this site builds the table for you.

  ### Decide your rule before you calculate

  Before running the numbers, decide how much upside you need to recommend a buy, so the result can't sway the rule.

  ### Starting point

  <div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="Starting point for the Costco valuation" tabindex="0">

  | | Value | Source |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Current share price | $1,000.00 | Illustrative |
  | Fiscal 2026 diluted EPS | $20.76 | Costco 10-K |
  | Current P/E | 48.17 | $1,000.00 ÷ $20.76 |

  </div>

  **Step 1: Forecast EPS.** Costco's EPS grew 10.0% in fiscal 2025 and 14.0% in fiscal 2026. A cautious analyst might assume 10% growth next year, the lower of the two:

  Forecast EPS = $20.76 × 1.10 = $22.84

  **Step 2: Choose a target P/E.** Comparable retailers (illustrative values):

  <div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="P/E ratios of comparable retailers" tabindex="0">

  | Company | P/E |
  | --- | --- |
  | Walmart | 38 |
  | BJ's Wholesale | 22 |
  | Target | 15 |
  | Average | 25.00 |
  | Median | 22.00 |

  </div>

  Costco trades far above its peers. Using the peer average of 25 would give a target of $571.00, a 42.9% drop, but that ignores why investors pay more for Costco: steady growth, a 92.3% renewal rate, and a strong balance sheet. This analyst chooses 45, a little below Costco's current 48.17, assuming its premium holds but shrinks slightly.

  **Step 3: Target price and upside.**

  Target price = $22.84 × 45 = $1,027.80

  Upside = ($1,027.80 − $1,000.00) ÷ $1,000.00 = 2.8%

  ### Testing the assumptions

  <div class="overflow-x-auto" role="region" aria-label="Costco target price at different target P/Es" tabindex="0">

  | Target P/E | Target price | Upside |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 40 | $913.60 | −8.6% |
  | 45 | $1,027.80 | 2.8% |
  | 50 | $1,142.00 | 14.2% |

  </div>

  **What this teaches.** With these assumptions, Costco looks close to fairly priced: a great company, but the price already reflects much of that. The answer swings from downside to strong upside depending on one choice, the P/E, so a real report would spend most of its valuation section defending that number. That's exactly the debate module 1 pointed to: is the stock too expensive for how fast the company is growing?

  All the numbers above follow the P/E calculator's rounding, so you can reproduce them there.

<Part id="do">
  1. **Look up your company's current share price** and write down the date.
  2. **Calculate its current P/E** using the latest diluted EPS from module 4.
  3. **Forecast next year's EPS.** Write your growth assumption and two reasons for it.
  4. **Pick three or four comparable companies**, look up each one's P/E, and write down the date and source.
  5. **Choose your target P/E**, and write two or three sentences explaining why.
  6. **Run the [P/E calculator](/tools/pe-calculator)** with your numbers. Copy the formula lines into your report.
  7. **Check the sensitivity table.** Note which inputs would flip your recommendation.
  8. **Draft your Valuation section:** your inputs, the math, your target price and upside, and the sensitivity table.
  9. **Tick off module 5** in your [progress tracker](/learn#progress) and share the draft with your mentor.
